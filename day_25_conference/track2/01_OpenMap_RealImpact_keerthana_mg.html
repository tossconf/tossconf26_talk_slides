India is complex — and that’s okay.

Theoretical models often fail when meeting the ground reality of Indian urban and rural landscapes.

1

Cluttered Rooftops

Indian roofs are multi-functional spaces with Sintex water tanks, clotheslines, and solar heaters casting heavy, dynamic shadows.

2

Map Coverage Gaps

Detailed building footprints exist for major cities like Chennai, but secondary cities and rural towns remain unmapped.

3

Weather & Dust Flux

Monsoons cause satellite data anomalies, while high urban dust (aerosols/soiling) lowers real panel performance by 15-30%.

4

Localized Projections

Measuring roof area accurately in meters requires specific local projection coordinates (UTM Zone 44N) that beginners struggle with.

No single organization can solve this alone.
This is where community-mapped data and open code step in.

10 Steps to Get Started

A beginner-friendly roadmap to building civic-tech and open-source solutions in India.

The Golden Rule

Start small.
Learn publicly.

Step 01

Map Your Neighborhood

Download the StreetComplete app or open OpenStreetMap, find your local road or favorite tea shop, and add it. Instant map impact!

Step 02

Write User Documentation

Help explain complex solar terms (like GHI vs. DNI) in simple language for developer READMEs and wiki pages.

Step 03

Fetch Local Weather Files

Use the free NASA POWER or PVGIS APIs to download Typical Meteorological Year (TMY) weather data for your home coordinates.

Step 04

Run a 10-Line Simulation

Write a short Python script using pvlib-python to model power output for a 1kW solar panel based on local coordinates.

Step 05

Compare Real vs. Model Data

Spot a weather anomaly? Log and document differences between satellite weather models and local weather station data.

Step 06

Clean Public Datasets

Write minor parsing or mapping scripts to format raw municipal or public CSV files into standardized, clean JSON.

Step 07

Build Interactive Map Tools

Use simple tools like Leaflet.js or MapLibre to display neighborhood outlines colored by solar potential.

Step 08

Translate Scientific Research

Translate academic papers on solar radiation models into readable utility functions and coding tutorials.

Step 09

Join Humanitarian Mapathons

Help map buildings in under-mapped towns during monsoons or floods using the HOT Tasking Manager.

Step 10

Share Your Open Source Project

Push your estimators/dashboards to GitHub, write a friendly readme, and share them with FOSS United or TOSS communities.
